A life spent in the lab

Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Marie Curie moved to Paris in 1891 to study physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne. Working with her husband Pierre, she studied the rays given off by uranium and coined the term "radioactivity."

In 1898 the pair announced two new elements: polonium, named for her homeland, and radium. She was the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

Her legacy

  • Developed mobile X-ray units in World War I.
  • Founded the Radium Institutes in Paris and Warsaw.
  • Her work helped lead to cancer radiotherapy.
